EDM · Micro-machining · Implant surfaces

Research

Six lines of work, all of them concerned with the same thing from different directions: what a controlled electrical discharge does to a metal surface, and how to predict it well enough to put a number on it.

Electrical discharge machining and its hybrid variants

EDM removes material with a spark rather than a cutting edge, which makes it indifferent to how hard the workpiece is — the reason it remains the practical route into nickel superalloys, titanium alloys and die steels. My work here has mostly been on hybrid variants, where EDM is combined with another mechanism to escape its usual limits: electro-discharge diamond grinding (abrasive action plus spark erosion), electrical discharge face grinding, powder-mixed and nano-emulsion dielectrics, and vibration assistance.

Materials we have worked through include Inconel 718, Waspaloy, Monel 400 and K-500, Nimonic 80A, AISI D2 and D3 die steels and Ti-6Al-4V. Representative papers: a review of electrical discharge grinding in Surface Review and Letters; tool-geometry and multi-objective optimisation studies on electric discharge drilling of Waspaloy in Materials and Manufacturing Processes and Alexandria Engineering Journal; and a non-edible-oil water-in-oil nano-emulsion dielectric in the Journal of the Brazilian Society of Mechanical Sciences and Engineering.

Micro-machining of advanced materials

At micro scale the discharge stops being a statistical average and starts being an individual event, so gap control, debris flushing and pulse discrimination decide whether a feature comes out round. We have worked on µ-EDM drilling, micro-electro-discharge milling and micro-WEDM, with low-frequency workpiece vibration as the main intervention — it improves flushing and, in our measurements, dimensional accuracy and surface quality of micro-channels and micro-holes. One strand of this, with C. K. Nirala, went into pulse discrimination from virtual signals, published in the ASME Journal of Manufacturing Science and Engineering. A later study established that the effect of workpiece vibration in µ-EDM drilling is essentially material-independent.

Surface engineering of biomedical implants

This is the Sheffield line, and the reason for the Marie Skłodowska-Curie project on β-phase titanium. An EDM-textured surface is not merely rougher: it is re-melted, re-solidified and chemically altered, which makes it a candidate for controlling two things clinicians care about at once — whether bone integrates with the implant, and whether bacteria colonise it. With Prof. G. C. Reilly, Prof. R. Goodall and F. Claeyssens we investigated EDM for surface modification of Ti6Al4V and the osteogenic response of EDM-machined surfaces after plasma treatment, presented at the European Society for Biomaterials conference and BioMedEng21. The review in Vacuum sets out the wider picture of thermal surface modification for corrosion and wear resistance of metallic implants, and related work covers hydroxyapatite-mixed EDM of biomaterials.

Sustainable machining: MQL and nanofluids

Flood coolant is effective and wasteful. Minimum quantity lubrication delivers a very small volume of fluid precisely, and the open question is what fluid. We have compared dry, flood and vegetable-oil MQL in CNC milling of Al 6061, and developed Ag/ZnO hybrid nanofluids for machining Inconel 718 and for micro-milling Ti6Al4V under nanofluid-based MQL, published in the Journal of Manufacturing Processes. The broader argument sits in the Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews paper on resource saving through optimisation and machining environment.

AI-driven modelling and optimisation

Machining data is noisy, non-linear and expensive to collect, which is exactly the regime where data-driven models earn their place. Across these processes we have built and compared artificial neural networks, fuzzy logic models, generalised regression neural networks, Gaussian process regression and response surface models, and coupled them to multi-objective optimisers — NSGA-II, MOGA, MOGWO, MOPSO, genetic algorithms, hybrid PSO-GA and Taguchi grey relational analysis. The point is rarely the algorithm; it is producing a parameter set an operator can actually set on a machine, with a defensible estimate of what it will deliver.

Thermal transport over machined surfaces

A side path that turned into a doctoral thesis. If EDM alters a surface at micron scale, it should alter how that surface boils a liquid. With Pravin Sharma we examined pool boiling enhancement over EDM-modified heater surfaces using ZnO and Ag/ZnO hybrid nanofluids, published in Colloids and Surfaces A and the Journal of Thermal Analysis and Calorimetry.

Earlier work: vehicle dynamics and control

My master's research at ARAI was on modelling and analysis of vehicle suspension, and that line continued for some years: skyhook, groundhook, fuzzy logic and ANFIS control strategies for semi-active suspension, quarter-car and seat-suspension models, and a two-wheeled self-balancing transporter. It is not the current focus, but it is where the modelling habits came from.
