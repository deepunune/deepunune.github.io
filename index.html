About

My fifteen years span academia, research and industry, and the industrial part came first. I began in the die shop at Bharat Forge, Pune, running CNC machines — HAAS, DMG MORI, MAKINO — on Fanuc, Fidia and Heidenhain controls, writing CAM programs and chasing die quality and tool life. That shop floor still shapes how I teach manufacturing: a process is not a diagram, it is a machine that wears, drifts and occasionally argues with you.

From there I moved to the Structural Dynamics Laboratory at the Automotive Research Association of India, Pune, then to teaching at Rajarambapu Institute of Technology, Islampur, and then to doctoral research at MNIT Jaipur, where I worked under Prof. Harlal Singh Mali on fabricating micro-features in Inconel 718 superalloy using hybrid EDM processes.

In 2019 the European Commission awarded me a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellowship, which took me to the Department of Materials Science & Engineering at the University of Sheffield for two years. The project — Electrical discharge machining for enhanced osseointegration and antibacterial capabilities of β-phase titanium implants, funded under EU Horizon 2020 at €224,933.76 — put me in a laboratory with Prof. G. C. Reilly and Prof. R. Goodall and changed the question I was asking. I had spent years on how precisely a surface could be machined. In Sheffield the question became what that machined surface does once it is inside a body: whether cells attach to it, whether bacteria do not, whether it corrodes.

Both questions still drive the group. On one side, precision: micro-EDM drilling and milling, wire EDM, electro-discharge diamond grinding, low-frequency vibration assistance, and the measurement work needed to say honestly how round a 200 µm hole really is. On the other, function: EDM-textured implant surfaces, corrosion and wear resistance, and the thermal behaviour of EDMed surfaces in pool boiling. Running through both is a third interest — using neural networks, fuzzy models, Gaussian process regression and multi-objective evolutionary algorithms to predict and optimise processes that are too noisy to model cleanly from first principles.

Since December 2024 I have led the Mechanical-Mechatronics Engineering department at LNMIIT, where the work is curriculum design, building laboratories, recruiting faculty and supervising research scholars. I teach machining and metal forming, CAD/CAM, production and operations management, and unconventional machining processes, and I have guided more than twenty B.Tech major projects alongside doctoral students.

Outside my own laboratory I organised the 23rd ISME International Conference on Recent Advances in Mechanical Engineering in 2025 and ASME EFx India 2026, which drew over 460 participants from 18 states, and I have reviewed more than 45 manuscripts for journals published by Elsevier, Springer, Taylor & Francis, MDPI, IOP, Sage and World Scientific since 2016.

I am glad to hear from prospective doctoral students, from industry groups with a machining problem that has resisted the obvious fixes, and from researchers working on implant surfaces or sustainable machining. Do get in touch.
